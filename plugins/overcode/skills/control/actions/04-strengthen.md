# Strengthen

Find the coverage gaps that actually matter in an existing suite, ranked by risk, and propose the smallest set of tests that closes the most exposure - never a bulk "raise coverage to N%" campaign.

Mirror image of `02-audit`: `audit` removes tests that carry no value, `strengthen` adds the few that carry the most. Both answer to the same two objects, which are not interchangeable: the `phase x domain-level` **ceiling** (`references/decision-matrix.md`), which refuses, and the **density** (`references/test-density.md`), which is reported alongside and says whether the cases sit in the right place. Both serve one principle: a gap is only worth a test when the risk it leaves open outweighs the maintenance cost of the test.

## Inputs

- `project_path` (required) - absolute path to the target project root
- `scope` (optional, default: whole project) - a subdirectory or glob to limit the analysis. **`scope` designates one universe, here as everywhere in this skill: the source code and the tests that match it.** The resolution is **symmetric** - a path landing in the source resolves down to its tests, a path landing in the test tree resolves up to the corresponding source, and the universe is the pair either way. No action has a universe of its own. What this action *ranks* is the source side of that pair; what bounds it is the pair.
- `domain` (optional) - a functional domain, resolved to code by the terms the project supplies, by **its own name**, by the terms `@../references/domain-catalogue.md` lists for it, and by the pivot's *Domain resolution* field - all four as literal case-insensitive substrings, no regex and no synonym expansion (`SKILL.md` › *Transversal rules*). Confirmed in `<project_path>/aidd_docs/memory/testing-domains.md` (`06-align`'s output) or **residue**: a name that file does not carry - or a project having no such file - is resolved per `SKILL.md` › *Transversal rules* (the name stands as declared by the argument; its **level** is the catalogue's default taken without a question where the name matches an entry, an open question where it does not, never persisted here), never collapsed into out-of-domain. It **weights the ranking; it never bounds the universe** - see step 2. Mutually exclusive with `scope` - given both, stop and ask which was meant (`SKILL.md`, *Parameters*). **One name, not a list** - given `domain=auth,payment`, stop and ask which was meant too: this action parses no multi-valued `domain`, and neither splitting it nor taking the first is a reading anyone asked for.
- `top_n` (optional, default: 5) - how many ranked gaps to report
- `phase` (optional) - overrides the resolved project phase for this run only

## Outputs

```
| rank | gap | risk_factors | cell | proposed_tier | why_it_matters |
|------|-----|--------------|------|---------------|----------------|
| 1 | src/billing/proration.js:computeRefund (branches 2/7 covered) | money, 6 branches, 4 fix-commits in 90d | production x critical - anchored nominal + degraded, ceiling 3, established 1 | e2e | the cell requires an anchored degraded proof and only the nominal one exists; a wrong branch silently refunds the wrong amount |
```

`cell` carries the `<phase> x <level>` it was read at, the **required proof**, the **ceiling** and what is **established** against it - the last obtained per `@../references/decision-matrix.md` › *Counting what is established*, rendered **per required term** on a conjunctive cell (`established 2 (anchored 0, internal 2)`) and as `unknown` when the tests could not be read, never as `0`. It is not decoration: `proposed_tier` is that cell's output name, so a row emitting the name without the cell emits a verdict with its authority left off - and this action emits more names per run than any other. **`why_it_matters` cites the cell**: the risk criteria explain why the row ranks where it does, the cell explains why the proof is the one proposed, and the two are not interchangeable.

```
unmatched: <count> term(s) resolving to nothing
  <one line each: <term> - <domain it was meant to resolve> - searched <where>>
unclassified: <count> source file(s) matched by no domain in force
  <one line each: <path> - nearest term that failed to recognise it>
```

**A miss is an output, not a footnote**, and it has a slot of its own so that an empty one is a statement. **The two slots cover opposite directions and neither substitutes for the other.** `unmatched` is the term's direction: a domain named `auth` in a codebase spelling it `identity` produces a term with no matches, and reporting the area as gap-free instead would be the exact inverse of the truth. `unclassified` is the code's: a file no domain in force recognises stays in the universe and stays rankable - it only ranks lower - and it is reported **with the term that came closest to naming it**, since a file dropping silently into the out-of-domain column is how a central part of a product gets declared unremarkable with nobody able to see it. Past the point where either list stops being readable, give the count and a representative sample, and say the list was cut - **never a silent truncation, and never a path to a file this action would have to write** (it writes nothing; the exhaustive list belongs to the run's own output or to a narrower `scope`).

**The side is named because the residue has two of them.** `unclassified` here is the **source** side - the same one `05-stats` reports at its step 1-quater; the **test** side is `02-audit`'s. `06-align` composes both as a single trace at its step 3 (*undeclared area*), so a half reported without its side stated is a half its own consumer cannot place, and two halves silently added together are a count of nothing.

Preceded by the resolved `phase` and its provenance, and by the **domains in force with their level and where that level came from** (`argument` / `testing-domains.md` / `catalogue - answered, not persisted` / `answered`) - the phase weights the ranking, the level is the matrix column every `cell` was read at, and a level whose provenance is left off is a column nobody can audit.

Followed by an explicit statement of what was deliberately **not** proposed and why (framework pass-throughs, generated code, paths already covered by an e2e journey), and - when any external contract boundary was found - of what is **out of reach of testing** and referred to monitoring.

No test file is written as part of producing this table.

## Process

1. Resolve `project_path` and `scope`. Load the target project's documented test strategy from the exact-case conventional path defined by `SKILL.md` › *The conventional strategy path is exact-case*, or from an explicitly named equivalent, else `@../references/decision-matrix.md`. A differently-cased sibling is reported and treated as absent consistently on every host. Detect the applicable language plugins and load the `testing` pivot of each one shipping it, per `@../references/pivot-contract.md`, for **Coverage command**, **Source glob & exclusions**, **Risk signals**, the test runner and the test file glob. **The source universe is the union of the contributed source globs, and the coverage figures are never merged across stacks** - one gap table per stack, each stated in its own stack's terms.

   Resolve the project **phase** per `@../references/phase-framework.md` and state it in the report header with its provenance (`argument` / `declared <path>` / `answered` / `unanswered`) - two distinct lines, never merged: the value says which phase, the provenance says where it came from. The phase is **never deduced**: when neither the argument nor the project's documentation gives it, **ask before ranking anything** - the answer is what the order is built on, so a table produced first and re-sorted after is a table the user has already read in the wrong order. The phase re-weights the criteria in step 3 - and it **classifies**, through the `phase x domain-level` cell it resolves: that cell states the required proof `proposed_tier` names and the ceiling a row can hit. What the phase still never does is move a `contract` to an `e2e` on a preference of its own, outside what the cell requires, nor change the bounded edge cases below.

   State which strategy is in force, **and which of the three document shapes was met** (`SKILL.md`, *Transversal rules*): absent, present but empty, or present and filled while settling no tier. The third is the one that misleads here - a document that names test types, tools and conventions looks like a strategy in force, and reporting it as such would credit the project with a tier decision it never took. Name the shape, then apply the fallback. When the project documents none, say so in the report and state the consequence plainly: no **cap** is declared. That is not the same as unbounded growth, and the reason is the ceiling, not the density: the resolved `phase x domain-level` **cell states its ceiling whatever the project declares** (`@../references/decision-matrix.md`) - a number, or `—` meaning no proof is required at that level and no ceiling applies - and where there is a number, that number is what refuses. **Report which of the two was read, never an absence of governance**: a `—` cell is an answer the matrix gives, not a gap in it, and rendering it as "no ceiling found" would read as a defect where the model is behaving exactly as specified. The **density** (`@../references/test-density.md`) is reported alongside and needs nothing declared to apply, its reference being the project's own median - but it prioritises and never refuses, so naming it as what bounds an undeclared project would put the refusal on the one instrument that cannot pronounce it. An undocumented project is reported as undocumented, never as implicitly following the default.

   Since this action's whole business is *adding*, the density is the one measurement that can argue against it, and it is read here in one specific way: a gap landing on a file already past 3× the median is not disqualified - it is reported with that fact attached. Many cases on little logic and a real gap remaining usually means the existing cases are aimed at the wrong thing, which makes it an `02-audit` referral first and an addition second. Say that; do not decide it. **Density ranks nothing and refuses nothing here either** - the risk criteria of step 3 own the order.

2. **Establish the universe to rank, then enrich it.** The **Source glob & exclusions** field pilots: it defines which files are classifiable production code. The coverage report only adds branch detail to that universe - it never defines it. `scope` narrows that universe; `domain` does not, and the difference is the whole reason the two are separate parameters.

   **Resolve the reading axes on the universe so established**, per `phase-framework.md`: `foundations` (the structural axis) and the domain-level of the domains in force, read from `<project_path>/aidd_docs/memory/testing-domains.md`. **A `domain` argument that file does not carry is residue, and its level is resolved before the ranking runs**, per `SKILL.md` › *Transversal rules* - the catalogue's default level taken without a question where the name matches an entry and cited as such, an open question where it matches nothing, neither persisted here and the output pointing to `06-align` to freeze it. Ranking a residual domain in the out-of-domain column would weight it by an absence of declaration rather than by what it is. A run with **no `domain` argument at all**, and no pivot *Domain resolution* match, runs the **out-of-domain** column of the matrix - read exactly like the other three, never a fallback carrying a substitute inventory of its own: no generic "critical journeys" list stands in for an absent domain. The pivot's **Domain resolution** field says how this stack expresses declared domains in paths and identifiers - it completes a domain named without a resolution, and never overrides one the project wrote about its own code. Without that field, resolve on the project's terms alone and say the resolution is unassisted.

   **A file matched by no domain in force is read in the out-of-domain column** - and that holds whether or not other domains are in force. The column classifies the **file**, not the run: keying it to "no domain in force at all" would leave every `unclassified` file without any cell to read, on precisely the runs where a domain exists, and a row cannot carry a `cell` for a column nobody assigned it. So a run with two domains in force and a residue reads **three** columns, and each row's `cell` names which one it came from.

   **A domain prioritises; it never restricts.** Code matching no domain stays in the universe and stays rankable - it only ranks lower. And **every term that resolved to nothing is reported**: a domain named `auth` that matches no file in a codebase which spells it `identity` would otherwise come out as an area with no gaps, which is the exact inverse of the truth. Report the miss with the term that produced it, and let the user decide whether the domain is clean or merely unfound.
   - Run the pivot's **Coverage command** as written and read per-file **branch/line** coverage. It is expected to produce a machine-readable report and to be decoupled from any coverage gate: a non-zero exit code from a threshold check is not a failure to read. Reason on `covered`/`total`, never on a percentage alone - a file with no branches reports 100% branch coverage while being entirely untested.
   - **A file matching the source glob but absent from the coverage report is uncovered, not nonexistent.** Reports frequently omit files no test ever imports, which is precisely the population most at risk; treating absence as coverage would hide exactly the gaps this action exists to find.

     **This reading is the same in every phase, deliberately.** It is a statement about how coverage reporters behave, not a weighting - and a phase that could change what an absence *means* would be deciding a measurement rather than prioritising one, which is the boundary the whole model rests on. What the phase legitimately changes is how high those files rank at step 3, and that is enough: in `scaffolding` a mass of never-imported files is the expected state and ranks low, in `sustaining` the same mass is the finding. Same fact read twice, never two facts.
   - No **Coverage command**, none configured in the project, or one whose **prerequisite is established as absent** (`@../references/pivot-contract.md` › *Prerequisites*): fall back to a static pass, mapping source modules to test files via the pivot glob and flagging modules no test references at all. State in the output that the ranking is static-only and therefore coarser - and, when the fallback was taken because a tool is missing rather than because nothing was measured, say which, since the two send the reader to fix opposite things.
   - **Where the pivot declares source and test non-disjoint, that static pass does not discriminate** and is not run in its place: every module hosting its own tests maps to itself, so the mapping flags either nothing or everything, and both readings are false. Report the stack as unrankable-by-static-mapping, naming the pivot's declared unit, and rank the remaining stacks normally - a coarse ranking is still a ranking, a degenerate one is a fabrication.
   - When no pivot is available, fall back to the project's own directory convention and say the universe is approximate.
3. Rank each candidate gap by risk, not by missing percentage. Coverage percentage is a symptom, never the target. Weight the six criteria below, **re-weighted by the resolved phase** per the table in `phase-framework.md` - the order given here is the neutral one, used as-is under `default` and `undetermined`. The two differ in what the report says, not in how it ranks: `default` is a neutrality the project chose and wrote down, and is reported as such; `undetermined` is a question left unanswered, and the report keeps saying so.
   - **Consequence** - the code handles money, auth/permissions, persistence, deletion, or anything else whose silent failure is not self-evident to a user. When the pivot supplies **Risk signals**, they are the stack-specific expression of this weight and take precedence over a generic reading. They **prioritise only, and within a domain**: a signal may move a gap to the top, or to the bottom, of its own domain's rows, never across domains, never off the table (step 4 owns exclusion, and this field is not one of its causes), and never change the tier proposed for it - that stays the output of the `phase x domain-level` cell (`@../references/decision-matrix.md`). The bound is the one `02-audit.md` already applies to the same field, and it is what keeps a measuring instrument from reversing the classifying authority: a signal able to lift an `ordinary` row above a `critical` one would be deciding the level rather than weighting inside it.
   - **Branching** - number of untested branches / conditions; a many-branch uncovered function outranks a long uncovered straight line.
   - **Churn** - frequency of recent changes, and how many of those commits were fixes, from the project's own git history. Code that keeps breaking is code that lacks a test.
   - **Blast radius** - how many callers depend on it.
   - **Absence of any other net** - no type check, no runtime validation, no e2e journey already crossing this path.
   - **External contract dependency** - the code depends on a third party's contract: an analytics or marketing SDK, a tag container, a transactional-email or CRM client, a payment SDK, an outgoing webhook, any client calling a domain the project does not control. The five criteria above are all internal, and none of them fires when the thing that breaks is the vendor - a major version can move, or a schema can change server-side, without a single line of the repository moving. Raised in `production`, **dominant in `sustaining`**, lowered in `scaffolding` where integrations are not wired yet. The stack-specific inventory comes from the pivot's **Risk signals**; without a pivot, read the project's manifest for dependencies pointing at domains it does not control, and say the inventory is generic.

     **Say what a test proves here, and what it does not.** Provable in process, at `contract` tier: the payload the code builds is the one it believes it is sending, and the **degraded path** behaves correctly when the vendor errors, returns an unexpected schema, or returns nothing. Not provable by the suite: that the vendor still accepts that payload - that needs a real, slow, quota-bound call. Report it as **out of reach of testing** and refer it to monitoring; never propose a test for it, which would trade a real gap for a false assurance.

     **Cost cap, per boundary.** One boundary is worth one test by default - the degraded path, and only when a vendor failure can interrupt the journey (blocking script, unhandled rejection, response schema consumed without a guard). An outbound-only integration whose failure is invisible client-side gets no test and is declared *monitored outside the test suite*. The built payload earns a second test only when it carries data with a verifiable in-process consequence: an amount, an order identifier, an authorisation status, a consent. This is a ceiling, not a quota - an integration may legitimately receive nothing.
4. Exclude from the ranking, and say so explicitly in the output:
   - everything the pivot's **Source glob & exclusions** declares non-classifiable (build artifacts, generated code, config, barrel re-exports, fixtures). Without a pivot, apply the generic equivalent - build output, vendored and generated code - and say the exclusion list is generic. **A *Risk signal* is never a ground for exclusion, whatever it says about a file.** That field orders, and dropping a row is the strongest classification available - stronger than naming a tier, since an excluded row carries no requirement at all and no trace in the output. `@../references/pivot-contract.md` does ask that field what is *structurally not* high-consequence in the stack, and the answer lands where every other signal lands: on the **Consequence** weight of step 3, pushing the row **down**. Down, and still on the table. Those files are usually reachable anyway through this bullet's own *Source glob & exclusions* or through the `skip` cell below, and where they are reachable through neither, ranking last is the whole of what a signal can do to them. Citing a *Risk signal* as the ground for a removal is the ordering field acting as the exclusion field, and it is a defect wherever it appears;
   - anything the resolved `phase x domain-level` cell classifies as `skip` (framework pass-throughs, unbranched assignments, a path an existing proof already exercises). **An empty `—` cell is not one of those causes**: it states that no proof is *required* at that level, never that one would be unwelcome, and excluding on that ground would let an absence of requirement act as a refusal - the one thing `@../references/decision-matrix.md` denies it. A gap landing in a `—` cell is ranked like any other, its row carrying `ceiling: none` (`01-write.md`, step 5, owns the same distinction at the sink);
   - paths already exercised end-to-end where the missing unit test would only re-assert what the journey already proves.

   **The three edge cases below each run at a moment other than the step they are written under.** They are grouped here for proximity; the order is stated because getting it wrong changes the output. **The empty-pair case runs at the close of step 2**, before any ranking - it suppresses the table entirely, so a table produced first and withdrawn after has already been read. **The at-ceiling case runs on the ranked rows**, after step 3 - it is a constat printed beside a row and it changes no order, so running it earlier would buy nothing and imply it could. **The saturation case** is read off step 3's output count, after ranking, for the same reason.

   **Edge case - no test in the resolved universe** (checked at the close of step 2). `scope` resolves symmetrically to a **pair** - the source and the tests that match it. When the **test side of that pair is empty** - a project with no test file at all, or a `scope` whose source side has no corresponding test tree - produce no ranking. Ranking every source file of the pair would deny the number constraint and turn this action into the bulk campaign it exists to prevent. Report the situation as a finding, **naming which side is empty and how the pair was resolved**, point at the strategy document (or its absence) as the thing to settle first, and stop.

   Two neighbouring cases are not this one and are never reported in the same words. **A pair whose test side is populated is analysed like any other**, whichever side `scope` was pointed at: a test-tree path resolves up to its source and is ranked normally, and reporting it as unable to yield a gap would make symmetric resolution a dead end. **And a `scope` that matched nothing at all** is a statement about the argument, not about the project - say which of the two was met, since an empty result and an argument that resolved to nothing look identical in the output and only one of them is good news.

   **Edge case - cell already at ceiling** (checked on the ranked rows). Read each ranked row's resolved `phase x domain-level` cell against `@../references/decision-matrix.md`. When it is already at ceiling - the total reached **and every required term satisfied**, per that file's *Counting what is established* - **report it plainly next to the row** (`cell at ceiling (n/n)`) - this is a **constat, never a derogation**: the gap stays in the ranked table exactly as any other, proposed on the same footing. Nothing here withholds a row, reassigns its domain, or downgrades its tier as a way around the ceiling - the refusal, if any, is `01-write`'s alone, decided when the row is actually handed off at step 7. This step only makes the fact visible earlier, where it is cheapest to read.

   **Edge case - saturation.** When the number of qualifying gaps vastly exceeds `top_n`, say so rather than presenting the top rows as if they were the whole story. Report the total, state that the ranking cannot be meaningful across a population that size, and propose narrowing `scope` to a subsystem the user can actually act on. **Never propose a `domain` as the remedy**: it reorders the same population and leaves it exactly as large, so it would answer a saturation with a table that merely looks shorter.
5. For each of the `top_n` gaps, propose a tier by reading the resolved `phase x domain-level` cell (same source and order as `01-write` step 3), and prefer **strengthening an existing test's assertion** over adding a new file whenever the code path already has a test - report that as the proposed action instead of a new test.
6. Present the ranked table. Nothing is written yet.
7. For each row the user explicitly confirms, hand it to `01-write` with the gap as `behavior` - so the tier decision, the number-constraint check and the delegation to `aidd-dev:06-test` all go through the single existing gate. Never bypass `01-write`, and never write a test for a row the user did not confirm. Rows go through **one at a time by default**; the two paragraphs below state the one case that lifts it and the guard that restores it.

   **A named batch on the addition side, margin-bounded.** A user may name a batch of *removals* and have it confirmed en bloc (`02-audit`), no bound attached. Additions are not alike, and the reason is that two different objects watch them. The **density** is the project's own moving median: every addition displaces it, so nothing can be counted against it in advance - and it never refuses anyway, it prioritises. The `phase x domain-level` **ceiling** is a fixed number, and a fixed number can be counted in advance. Hence the bound: **a user-named addition batch is admissible as long as it fits entirely within the remaining margin of every cell it touches** - none of its members would then have been approved against a constraint another member pushed it past. Announce the **total** and the **margin** together before the first handoff, lot or not - a batch refused for exceeding its margin states by how much.

   **Cumulative guard.** Past the margin, rows pass through `01-write` **one at a time**, the cell ceiling and any project-declared cap both re-evaluated between each, the density re-read alongside - the rule degrades on its own as a cell fills, down to line-by-line at saturation.

## Test

**Referred by file, never by row number.** A row list goes stale on the next renumbering of a suite, and a stale list is worse than none - it points a reader at a scenario that now tests something else. Each suite below declares the actions it targets in its own opening paragraph; that declaration is the authority for this list, and it is the thing to re-read when a suite is added or re-scoped.

Covered by `../evals/matrix-scenarios.md`, `../evals/measurement-scenarios.md`, `../evals/domains-scenarios.md`, `../evals/phase-scenarios.md`, `../evals/authority-scenarios.md`, `../evals/confirmations-scenarios.md` and `../evals/chaining-scenarios.md`.

Seven suites reach this action, which is the widest surface in the skill: the cell and its ceiling (`matrix`), the density and its degenerate cases (`measurement`), the domains in force (`domains`), what the phase may bound (`phase`), what may decide and what may only measure (`authority`), the batch regime on a proposed test (`confirmations`), and the graph position (`chaining`).

Run: `overcode:behave 02-run <suite> <fixture>`.
